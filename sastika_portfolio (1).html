Riveted Composite Structures Under Static & Impact Loading: A Comparative FEA Study

🏫 CSULB · Instructor: Surajit Roy

Numerically investigated the effect of rivet configuration (1-, 2-, and 4-rivet layouts) on interlaminar damage in GFRP–Nomex honeycomb sandwich panels under both quasi-static and high-velocity impact loading. Two complementary FEA approaches were employed in parallel.

Case Study 1 - ANSYS ACP (Static): Modelled a 12-ply quasi-isotropic GFRP laminate using ANSYS Composite PrepPost (ACP). A bilinear Cohesive Zone Model (CZM) was introduced at the laminate mid-plane (between plies 6 and 7) using the Quadratic Nominal Stress (QNS) damage initiation criterion and Benzeggagh–Kenane (BK) mixed-mode fracture propagation law. Both force-controlled (20 kN tensile) and displacement-controlled (5 mm remote) loading were applied. The 2-rivet layout delivered the most balanced response - 21% reduction in von Mises stress vs. 1-rivet, with controlled delamination growth.

Case Study 2 - LS-DYNA (Impact): Simulated high-velocity transverse impact (900 m/s rigid spherical impactor, 5 mm radius) on a GFRP–Nomex sandwich panel using explicit dynamics. Facesheet–core bonding was modelled via tiebreak contact formulation. The 4-rivet configuration reduced the debonded interface area by ~70% compared to the 1-rivet case, with core crushing volume cut by over 50%, confirming that higher fastener density is critical under impact loading.

Parametric Analysis of T/C Ratio Influence on Scramjet Compression Using Diamond Airfoil Geometries

🔬 CFD Research · COMSOL Multiphysics

Investigated how the thickness-to-chord (t/c) ratio of a double-wedge diamond airfoil affects shock-based aerodynamic compression for scramjet inlet pre-compression at Mach 2.0. Four t/c ratios (0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4) were evaluated using 2D compressible CFD simulations, validated against classical oblique shock theory via the θ–β–M relation and Rankine–Hugoniot jump conditions.

Analysis targeted three competing requirements: maximizing total pressure recovery, achieving hydrogen auto-ignition temperature (~452 K), and maintaining shock-on-lip alignment for 100% mass capture. The t/c = 0.3 configuration emerged as optimal - delivering a stable attached oblique shock, precise cowl alignment, and sufficient post-shock heating without sacrificing aerodynamic stability.

SA4: Lead-Lag Compensator Design for Type-1 Plant

🏫 CSULB · Controls · SA4

Designed a combined lead-lag compensator for the plant Gp(s) = 10 / [s(s+2)(s+5)] meeting two hard specs: dominant closed-loop poles at sd = -2 ± j2√3 and velocity constant Kv = 50 (ramp ess = 0.02). Lead compensator placed the zero at s = -1 and used root locus angle geometry to compute the pole at s ≈ -8.8, with gain K_lead ≈ 13.44. Lag compensator used β = 10 (within the allowed 3-10 range) with pole at s = -0.01 and zero at s = -0.10, yielding K_lag ≈ 3.27 > 1. Full compensated system confirmed Kv = 50.000. Simulated root locus, step response (52% overshoot, 5.05 s settling), and ramp error converging to ess = 0.020.
